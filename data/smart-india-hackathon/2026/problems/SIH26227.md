**Background:** Earth-observation archives are expanding rapidly and increasingly contain multi-temporal, multi-spectral and multi-sensor imagery. Conventional catalogues are effective for searching by metadata such as coordinates, acquisition date, platform and product type, but analysts may still need to know where and when to look before they can examine the imagery itself. Recent advances in multimodal and remote-sensing foundation models have improved semantic representation of Earth-observation data, creating the possibility of searching imagery by meaning as well as metadata. Translating those advances into a reliable operational system remains challenging, particularly when the system must work on-premises, ingest new acquisitions incrementally, preserve geospatial provenance, and suppress false change caused by season, atmosphere, viewing geometry, registration error or sensor differences.

**Description:** Teams are required to build a system that makes a satellite-imagery archive queryable by semantic content and by change over time, while retaining conventional spatial, temporal and sensor filters. The solution should support analyst discovery rather than require the analyst to identify every location of interest in advance. The system must demonstrate six core capabilities:
1.  **Semantic and Multimodal Retrieval:** Support free-text search over imagery tiles using natural-language queries, together with image-to-image search for visually and semantically similar locations. Results should be rank ordered and may be refined using metadata filters.
2.  **Multi-Temporal Change Analysis:** For a specified area and time window, identify meaningful changes such as appearance, disappearance, expansion or contraction of features; classify supported change types; and estimate the earliest available observation at which the change is supported.
3.  **False-Alarm Suppression and Quality Handling:** Seasonal variation, illumination, cloud, haze, and other confounding factors must be treated to suppress false changes. The system should favour analytically useful precision over indiscriminate change recall.
4.  **Discovery and Clustering:** Support unsupervised or embedding-based grouping of similar sites across a wider area so that an analyst can discover other locations with comparable characteristics.
5.  **Analyst Workflow and Provenance:** Provide a ranked review queue with before-and-after evidence. Analysts should be able to confirm or reject candidates, preserve decisions in an audit trail, and use feedback for refinement. Exported results must retain source-scene and processing provenance.
6.  **Scale, Incremental Ingestion and Sovereignty:** Support efficient vector indexing, incremental addition of new imagery, and complete on-premises operation. The solution should ingest common geospatial formats like GeoTIFF or COG.

**Expected Solution:** A working system will be demonstrated over an organiser-defined area of interest and time span using public imagery. Retrieval will be evaluated against held-out semantic queries, while change analysis will be evaluated against a held-out set of labelled cases. Teams must submit source code, an architecture note, the index-build and incremental-ingestion procedure, model and dataset provenance, and a reproducible evaluation report stating performance metrics.

**Dataset Link:** Primary Imagery Sources: Copernicus Sentinel-2 optical imagery and Sentinel-1 SAR; USGS Landsat Collection 2; and NRSC/ISRO Bhuvan open Earth-observation data products.
